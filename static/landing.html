01 / Approach

The problem,
and the hack

The locked-away truth

Accurate elevation underpins disaster response, urban planning, agriculture, and infrastructure. But useful terrain data can be expensive, difficult to source, or unavailable when you need it most.

The decode

A single nadir image contains more about terrain shape than it appears to. DepthWizard extracts a per-pixel depth map with a fine-tuned depth model, calibrates it to real-world elevation, anchors it against SRTM terrain, and produces a WGS84 GeoTIFF DSM with an interactive 3D view.

Built to be practical

No specialized survey hardware. No manual DEM processing. Upload an image, provide its location, and let the pipeline handle inference, calibration, georeferencing, and export in one workflow.

03 / Pipeline

One image,
five hardened stages

01

Georeference

Reads CRS + transform from a GeoTIFF, or accepts an optional WGS84 bounding box, with relative-only mode when geographic metadata is unavailable.

02

Tiled depth inference

A GAMUS fine-tuned Depth-Anything-V2 model predicts depth per pixel. Overlapping 518px tiles are stitched with cosine-feather blending for a continuous structural map.

03

Metric calibration

A robust RANSAC fit converts relative depth into metric structural height. 1–2 known points can be added in the viewer to refine the calibration for the scene.

04

Terrain baseline

30 m SRTM ground elevation is fetched on demand and resampled onto the image grid, providing the terrain baseline beneath the predicted structures.

05

DSM + export

Absolute DSM = SRTM terrain + calibrated structure, exported as a Float32 WGS84 GeoTIFF. Results are rendered interactively in Three.js and Deck.gl with region statistics.

Honesty

What we don't
claim

Built for urban

The model is fine-tuned on the GAMUS urban dataset. Forest canopy is only partially captured, while flat surfaces with little relief provide limited depth signal.

Relative until calibrated

Raw estimates are relative until calibrated to known elevations. Adding a few known heights can substantially improve elevation accuracy, depending on the scene and calibration data.

We measure, then decide

Segmentation and piecewise calibration were tested but not promoted because their gains stayed below our 5 to 10% improvement threshold. Not every experiment needs to ship.
